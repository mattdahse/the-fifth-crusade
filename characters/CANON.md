# Canonical Character Portraits

This directory is the **source of truth for how the people of the chronicle look.**
Every named character who has been given a face lives here as a single canonical
portrait. When any image is generated that depicts one of these characters — a
chapter illustration, a scene, a group shot — that character's canonical portrait
below **must** be supplied to the image tool as a likeness reference, so they stay
recognizable from picture to picture. All new art follows the house look described in
[`../bible/04-visual-style-guide.md`](../bible/04-visual-style-guide.md).

The portraits were gathered in different styles; that is fine for a reference of
*who a character is*. The style guide governs *how* new art is rendered. The
definitive style exemplar is the full-body Arueshalae in [`../images/arueshalae.webp`](../images/arueshalae.webp).

**Identity here, gear elsewhere.** This file fixes each character's *unchanging* identity —
face, build, coloring, species markers. Their **gear changes as the story advances**, so
armor/weapons/relics by era live in [`../bible/05-kit-and-timeline.md`](../bible/05-kit-and-timeline.md).
When you illustrate a scene, take the **face from here** and the **kit from the era block
there** (e.g. early Book I ≠ the later golden-plate look). Where a per-era note here and the
kit guide disagree, the kit guide wins.

## Known drift — state the negatives explicitly in every prompt

Image models fall into the same traps repeatedly. Descriptions here are **prescriptive**: if an anchor is silent on something, the model invents it, and it invents these:

- **Fantasy-armor sexualization.** Any woman in "leather" trends toward a bikini, bare midriff, or strap harness. Arueshalae in particular is a succubus, which pulls hard in that direction — she is a *penitent*, dressed as a working scout in covered leathers. Always say **"fully covered, modest, practical armor"** and add `bare midriff, revealing outfit, bikini armor, cleavage` to the Avoid line. **The one deliberate exception is Areelu Vorlesh**, who is written to flaunt her emerging succubus nature — do **not** reflexively cover her up, and do **not** put the covered-armor negatives above in her Avoid line. Her canonical costume *is* a strap-and-chain harness; see her row for the specifics, and hold the line only at `nudity, explicit content`.
- **Orc caricature.** "Half-orc" alone yields tusks, an underbite, and a brutish snout. Harlock has none of these. Say **"no tusks, no underbite, clean strong human-like jaw"** and put `tusks, underbite, protruding lower teeth, brutish features` in the Avoid line.
- **Child-like characters must never be sexualized** — but say so **positively, never as negatives.** Any prompt depicting a child (Cornelia, or a PC rendered young in a dream) describes the clothing as **"simple, sturdy, plain and neat, long-sleeved, fully covering everyday clothing"** and the figure as **"a child with youthful childlike proportions."** **Do NOT put `sexualized`, `bare torso`, `bare midriff`, `adult proportions`, or `revealing clothing` in the Avoid line of a prompt depicting a child.** Safety classifiers do not parse negation: those words sitting beside a child's description read as intent and get the whole prompt refused before it renders. (This is the reverse of the rule for adult women like Arueshalae, where the explicit negatives are required — see the entry above.) Learned the hard way on Rabiah's `beneath-the-stairs`, which was refused at the prompt stage on the first attempt.
- **Deities are not weathered mortals.** A god depicted in the chronicle **emits a subtle divine glow** and their raiment, armor, and weapons are **flawless — no dent, smudge, soot, rust, tatter, or battle damage — no matter how ruined the setting around them.** The contrast against a crumbling, filthy world is the point, and the glow is the image's single luminous accent. **Only a deity's face may bear scars**, as a remnant of a mortal life (Iomedae's do; her armor never does). Left unsaid, the model will distress a god's gear to match the environment. Applies to Iomedae, Sarenrae, and Desna alike.
- **Human ears drift pointed when an elf stands nearby.** Rabiah is human, but her row long omitted any ear description, and the model kept giving her Lupenor's long elf ears — the two share copper-red hair and freckles, so the silhouette collapses into one character. State a human's ears **positively** (**"small rounded human ears, not pointed"**) for any human who could be confused with an elf, and put `pointed ears, elf ears` in the Avoid line. The rule generalizes: when two characters share coloring, name the feature that separates them in *both* their rows rather than trusting the species label to carry it.
- **Inferred lore is not observed detail.** Rabiah's row once claimed a "Riftwardens' spiral at the shoulder" because the *cast bio* mentions the spiral — the portrait shows a plain round medallion. Describe only what the PNG shows; keep lore out of the likeness anchors.
- **Radiance is a SWORD, not a shield.** The relic ***Radiance*** is Harlock's intelligent golden longsword; his shield is an unnamed piece bearing Iomedae's sunburst (a wooden one in early Book I). Prompts kept turning the "blazing star" into a named shield in his hand. Say **"an intelligent golden longsword"** for the relic, and — in any Book I scene — describe the blade as **dimmed, dull and giving off no light** (it does not glow until it awakens). Put `glowing sword, blazing shield` in the Avoid line for early-Book-I scenes.
- **Iomedae's device is a sword before a sun — Sarenrae's is a bare sunburst.** Left to itself the model gives every holy symbol in the frame the same generic starburst, and Harlock and Varic stop being distinguishable at a glance. **Iomedae: a vertical sword, point down, set in front of a blazing sun.** Spell out the sword every time. **This is the goddess's symbol, not one man's** — it belongs to **every** Iomedaean and every Iomedaean object: Harlock's breastplate and shield, **Irabeth's** breastplate and pauldrons, the Eagle Watch's banners and shields, the altars and devices of her temples. **Sarenrae: the plain starburst / stylized sun, no sword** — correct for Varic, and it stays generic. Put `a bare starburst with no sword` in the Avoid line of any scene with an Iomedaean in it. *(Legacy drift, decided and closed: the pre-existing Book I art and Irabeth's own portrait show a bare sunburst. Those stand. Everything new carries the sword.)*
- **The model reaches for a CHRISTIAN CROSS whenever a scene smells of crusaders, tombs or ritual — and Golarion has none.** This is not the same drift as the bare-starburst note above: it invents a real-world religious symbol out of nothing, and it does it unprompted. *(Book III Ch. III, Aug 2026: within one session a sacrificial cultist came back wearing a point-down sword-cross pendant, and a crusader ghost came back in a white surcoat bearing a plain Latin cross.)* Any figure who is a **crusader, paladin, priest, tomb, banner or surcoat** needs its device named explicitly — **Iomedae's sword-point-down-before-a-sun**, **Sarenrae's bare sunburst**, **Shelyn's songbird** — and any figure who should carry **no** device needs that said too (*"nothing at his chest: no pendant, no medallion, no chain, no holy symbol"*). Put `a cross, a crucifix, a Latin cross, a cross on a surcoat or tabard, a Christian symbol` in the Avoid line of **every** prompt containing armoured religious figures, whether holy or profane. Silence is what produces the cross.
- **Verify against the portrait, not your memory of it.** Both errors above originated in this file — an anchor written from a hazy recollection propagated straight into the art. Re-open the PNG before you write or trust a row.
- **Never record a drift note from a comparison you did not actually make.** A whole entry in this section was once written claiming that asking for a more revealing costume also zoomed the framing and hollowed the face. Neither had happened: the two renders had been judged against a remembered version of an image that had never been opened, and when all of them were finally viewed side by side the face and framing were identical. **Open every image in the comparison, then write the note.** A fabricated finding is worse than no finding — it hardens into a rule, and every later prompt pays to prevent a problem that does not exist.
