# Last Will of Trever Vaenic

*Written by Trever Vaenic of Lastwall — a crusader some years dead — in the hour before he went alone into the woundwyrm's lair, and carried in on his own body, so that it came back out of Scorizscar's hoard with the rest of what she had taken. He directed it to his brother at Vigil, in a season when Drezen was a demon-held ruin nobody expected to see again, and no letter could have been delivered there by anyone living. It reached Sosiel Vaenic at last in Drezen, carried by hand, by a man who would not trust it to a courier. The sheet was stiff and brown and had to be parted a leaf at a time; in the cave nothing could be read of it but the direction.*

To whoever finds this sheet:

Get it out of the **Worldwound**. I do not care how long that takes you or what you have to do about it. Carry it to **Vigil**, in **Lastwall**, and ask at any house of *Shelyn* for **Sosiel Vaenic** — he was a priest there when I marched, and if he has been moved on they will know where to. He is my brother.

He will want to pay you for the trouble and you should let him, because he is no use to anybody until he has done something with his hands. If he has no coin he will bless you instead and believe he has got the better end of it. Let him believe it.

I know what the odds are on this paper ever reaching him. They are worse than mine, and I have already worked out mine.

Sosiel.

We were eleven and four wagons, three weeks east of the last crusader post I could put a name to. We had been sent up to see what could be made out of **Drezen** from the high ground — whether anything was still standing, whether anything could ever be done about it. Nobody in the column believed anything could. I want that written down too, so that you know we went anyway.

It came at us out of a clear sky in the eighth hour. I am going to set down what it was, because nobody who was not standing there will believe it, and I would rather it was written by a man who saw it.

It has no colour. I have tried three times now to put a colour on it and there is not one — it is dark, and it shifts, and the eye slides off it like a wet stone. It has six legs. I know how that reads. Six. It came over us low and the heat of it went through my helm the way an oven door opens on your face, and where it set down the rock smoked, and went on smoking after it had gone.

I lived because the first pass threw me into the ditch under a wagon bed, and because I had the sense to stay there. I am not going to dress that up for you.

It did not kill all of them. That is the whole reason for this letter and I need you to hold onto it. It killed **Ansel** and it killed the drivers and it killed every horse on the line, and then it took four of them up alive and went west with them. **Kett Harrow**. **Doran Vey**. The Iomedaean girl out of **Nerosyan** whose name I am ashamed to tell you I never once asked for. And one more I could not see. They were alive. They were shouting. I heard them going away over the flats and I lay in that ditch and let it happen, because there was not one thing in this world I could have done from under a wagon.

I have followed it three days west. Acid burned into the rock, and long gouges, and the leavings of everything this creature has pulled off these roads for longer than I have been alive, lying out in the open where it dropped them. There is a dry riverbed through this country — our maps had it as the Gray Road, for whatever a crusader map is worth out here — and down in the bottom of it is a field of broken wagons, and past that a cave mouth in the western bank with the ground outside burned white. That is where it dens. I am writing this propped against rock a quarter mile short of it, and when I have finished I am going in.

I thought about leaving this under a cairn out here where an honest traveller might turn it up. But nothing in the **Worldwound** stays where it is put, and there are no honest travellers in it. So I am carrying it in with me. Which means that if you are holding this sheet, you have been inside that cave yourself, and you already know how it went for me. I am sorry you had to see it.

I know exactly what I am. I am one man with a sword and I am not coming out of there. But they were alive four days ago, and they will not be alive by the time anyone else so much as hears of it, and I am the only man breathing who knows they are in there at all. There is no arithmetic in that. There is just the fact of it.

So — the will, since I have gone and called it one. There is no property, which will not surprise you. Whatever season's pay is still owed me, take it and give it to whichever of your gods will put food in front of people fastest. Our father's ring is in the ground at home and I never told you which stone; it is the third from the wall, and I have carried that badly for three years, and I am sorry. And I am taking ***Battle Hymn*** in with me. It was forged to bite things out of the Abyss and it is not going to do that hanging on a temple wall in **Vigil**.

Do not come out here after me. You would not last a week and you know it, and I would rather the last argument I ever win with you be that one. Do not make a production of it either. You will want to paint something, and you will be no good at anything for a while, and then you will be, and I would take it kindly if you got to the second part quickly.

You were right about the beauty of it, by the way. I have spent three years out here arguing with you inside my own head and you were right and I was not, and I was never going to write that down until I had a reason.

Your brother,

**Trever Vaenic**
