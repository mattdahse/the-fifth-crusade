# Last Will of Trever Vaenic

*Written by Trever Vaenic, crusader out of Drezen, in the hour before he went into the woundwyrm's lair alone — and carried in on his own body, so that it came back out of Scorizscar's hoard with the rest of what she had taken. The sheet was stiff and brown and had to be parted a leaf at a time before any of it could be read; in the cave nobody could make anything of it but the direction. On the taking of the Kenabres caravans, on four crusaders who were carried off alive, and on the sword called Battle Hymn.*

<!-- letter: hand -->

To whoever finds this sheet:

Carry it to **Sosiel Vaenic**, priest of *Shelyn*, at **Drezen**. Ask at the temple that serves all the good gods and someone will walk you to him. He is my brother. He will want to pay you for the trouble and you should let him, because he is no use to anybody until he has done something with his hands. If he has no coin he will bless you instead and believe he has got the better end of it. Let him believe it.

Sosiel.

We were eleven out of **Drezen** on the **Kenabres** road with four wagons. It came at us out of a clear sky in the eighth hour, and I am going to set down what it was, because no one who was not standing there is going to believe it, and I would rather it was written by a man who saw it.

It has no colour. I have tried three times now to put a colour on it and there is not one — it is dark, and it shifts, and the eye slides off it like a wet stone. It has six legs. I know how that reads. Six. It came over us low and the heat of it went through my helm the way an oven door opens on your face, and where it set down the rock smoked, and went on smoking after it had gone.

I lived because the first pass threw me into the ditch under a wagon bed, and because I had the sense to stay there. I am not going to dress that up for you.

It did not kill all of them. That is the whole reason for this letter, and I need you to hold onto it. It killed **Ansel** and it killed the drovers and it killed every horse on the line, and then it took four of them up alive and went west with them. **Kett Harrow**. **Doran Vey**. The Iomedaean girl out of **Nerosyan** whose name I am ashamed to tell you I never once asked for. And one more I could not see. They were alive. They were shouting. I heard them going away over the flats and I lay in that ditch and let it happen, because there was not one thing in this world I could have done from under a wagon.

I have followed it three days west. Acid burned into the rock, and long gouges, and the leavings of every caravan that road has lost these two years lying out in the open where it dropped them. The dry riverbed the drovers call the **Gray Road** runs through here, and there is a field of broken wagons down in the bottom of it, and past that a cave mouth in the western bank with the ground outside burned white. That is where it dens. I am writing this propped against rock a quarter mile short of it, and when I have finished I am going in.

I thought about leaving this under a cairn out here, where an honest traveller might turn it up. But nothing on this road stays where it is put, and there are no honest travellers left on it. So I am carrying it in with me. Which means that if you are holding this sheet, you have been inside that cave yourself, and you already know how it went for me. I am sorry you had to see it.

I know exactly what I am. I am one man with a sword and I am not coming out of there. But they were alive four days ago, and they will not be alive by the time word reaches **Drezen** and **Drezen** finds anyone to send, and I am the only man breathing who knows they are in there at all. There is no arithmetic in that. There is just the fact of it.

So — the will, since I have gone and called it one. There is no property, which will not surprise you. My season's pay is with the quartermaster; give it to whichever of your gods will put food in front of people fastest. Our father's ring went into the ground at **Kenabres** and I never told you, and I have carried that badly for two years, and I am sorry. And I am taking ***Battle Hymn*** in with me. It was forged to bite things out of the Abyss and it is not going to do that hanging on a wall in your temple.

Do not come out here looking for me. Do not make a production of it. You will want to paint something and you will be no good at anything for a while, and then you will be, and I would take it kindly if you got to the second part quickly.

You were right about the beauty of it, by the way. I have spent two years out here arguing with you inside my own head and you were right and I was not, and I was never going to write that down until I had a reason.

Your brother,

**Trever Vaenic**
