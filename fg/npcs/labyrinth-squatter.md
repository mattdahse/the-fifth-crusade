## Description

> A man of about forty, unshaven, hair going grey at the temples, with a nose that was broken
> once and set by somebody who did not care. His scale mail is brown with rust and he wears it
> over a gambeson you can smell from a spear's length. There is a hooked iron disc on a thong at
> his throat, stamped with a maze. He is holding the axe low, and he is looking at you the way a
> man looks at weather.
>
> He keeps himself between you and the east door, and he does not once put his back to the room's
> north-east corner, where the floor has a hatch in it.

## Special abilities

None. He is a man in bad armour with an axe, and that is the whole of it.

## Tactics

He fights like someone who intends to still be alive at the end of it, which makes him more
dangerous than his numbers and easier to get rid of than his numbers.

- **He does not open.** Left alone he holds a doorway and makes the party come to him.
- **He gives ground**, hurt past about half, backing toward the road rather than trading — but
  **never toward the hatch**, and he will take a bad position to avoid going that way.
- **He will not die for the acolyte.** If she goes down, he stops.
- **Two together are the problem**, because neither has to worry about being flanked.

## Roleplaying

He came north for pay under the Templars of the Ivory Labyrinth, and the maze token at his throat
is a meal ticket rather than a faith: it says any house keeping the mark owes him food and a roof.
Ask him about **Baphomet** and he will tell you what the mark gets him, and be puzzled at the
question underneath.

**He is a survivor of his own warband.** Nineteen men came to this house. He is one of two left,
and the others did not desert and did not die in a fight. They argued with the acolyte, or they
tried to walk south in the night, and they went down the hatch in the east room, and the house
was quieter afterwards. He has watched this happen eleven times. The last one was three weeks ago
and he knew the man.

**He can absolutely be bought**, and more cheaply than he should be: a few gold, a road south, or
a credible promise that nobody comes looking. He will take coin over a fight every time the offer
is made before the first blow, and about half the time after.

**He is looking for an excuse.** Not to betray her — he is nowhere near brave enough to plan
that — but to be somewhere else when it happens. Give him a door and he uses it, and he tells the
party one true thing on the way through, because he would rather be owed a favour than remembered
as a problem.

**What he will not do is go down that hatch.** Not for money, not under threat, not to save his
own life. This is the hardest fact about him and it should not bend: a party that tries to force
him will find he would genuinely rather be killed in the room he is standing in. If they ask him
what is down there he will tell them for free, in about nine words, and watch them go with the
expression of a man watching weather.
