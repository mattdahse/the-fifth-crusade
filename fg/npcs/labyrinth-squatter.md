## Description

> A man of about forty, unshaven, hair going grey at the temples, with a nose that was broken
> once and set by somebody who did not care. His scale mail is brown with rust and he wears it
> over a gambeson you can smell from a spear's length. There is a hooked iron disc on a thong at
> his throat, stamped with a maze. He is holding the axe low, and he is looking at you the way a
> man looks at weather.

## Special abilities

None. He is a man in bad armour with an axe, and that is the whole of it. Nothing here needs
looking up mid-fight.

## Tactics

He fights like someone who intends to still be alive at the end of it, which makes him more
dangerous than his numbers and easier to get rid of than his numbers.

- **He does not open.** Left alone he holds a doorway or a wall and makes the party come to him.
  The house rewards that and he knows the house.
- **He gives ground.** Hurt past about half, he backs toward the rubble or the road rather than
  trading. He will not turn his back while anyone is still swinging.
- **He will not die for the acolyte.** If she goes down, or if she has already spent the party's
  patience, he stops. Nothing about the arrangement is worth this to him.
- **Two together are the problem.** Alone he is a speed bump. With another squatter holding the
  same doorway he is a real fight, because neither has to worry about being flanked.

## Roleplaying

He came north for pay and stayed because there is nothing to go back to. The Ivory Labyrinth's
mark at his throat is a meal ticket, not a faith — it says any house keeping the mark owes him
food and a roof, and that is the entire extent of his religion. Ask him about the Labyrinth and
he will tell you what it gets him, and be puzzled at the question underneath.

**He can absolutely be bought.** Not with much, either: a few gold, a way south, or a credible
promise that nobody will come looking for him. He will take coin over a fight every time the
offer is made before the first blow, and roughly half the time after.

**He is looking for an excuse.** Not to betray the acolyte exactly — he is not brave enough to
plan that — but to be somewhere else when it happens. A party that gives him a door out will
find he uses it, and that he tells them one true thing on the way through, because he would
rather be owed a favour than remembered as a problem.

**What he will not do** is walk into that cellar. He has heard what is under the house at night
and he has decided, firmly and permanently, that it is not his business. No amount of money
changes this. If the party wants to know what is down there, he will tell them for free, and
watch them go with the expression of a man watching weather.
