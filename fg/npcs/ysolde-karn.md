## Description

> A lean woman in her thirties, dark hair scraped back hard and pinned, pale eyes that have
> already finished looking at you. Plain studded leather over dark wool, no colours, no badge — a
> heavy mace at her belt and an iron hook lashed over her left hand with cord worn into the
> wrist. When she does not believe you, that hand comes up an inch without her deciding to.
>
> There is a small bronze thing pinned inside the breast of her coat where a badge would go if
> she wanted one seen. It is a locust, wings spread.

## Special abilities

**Adept spells, CL 2nd, concentration +5.** The full set is on the Spells tab and the cast
buttons work; these are the two that change the fight.

- ***Burning hands*** — 1d4 fire in a 15-foot cone, Reflex DC 14 for half. Indoors, under a roof,
  with the party bunched in a doorway, this is the most dangerous thing in the house.
- ***Cure light wounds*** — on herself, at about half, and early rather than heroically late.

**She has no power over the ants.** This matters and it is not on the sheet. Nothing in her
statblock commands vermin, and nothing in it protects her from them. If the fight goes into the
cellar and she calls them off, they do not stop.

## Tactics

She is a sergeant, not a duellist, and she fights like one.

- **She sets the ground before initiative.** Given warning, she puts the squatters in the doorway
  and stands behind them where she can see the whole room.
- **She opens with the cone** if the party obliges by bunching, and holds it for several rounds
  rather than waste it on one target.
- **She does not close.** The mace is for when something reaches her.
- **She heals herself, not them.**
- **Losing, she goes for the hatch**, not the road — and this is the moment the encounter turns.
  She goes down there believing she is retreating into her own house. Let her. Play it straight.

## Roleplaying

She serves **Deskari**, Lord of the Locust Host, and she has served him quietly inside somebody
else's warband for a year. The men she commands wear **Baphomet's** maze on a thong at the throat
and were sent here by the Templars of the Ivory Labyrinth to count crusader traffic on the
western road. The two lords are allies; nobody in the Labyrinth thought twice about attaching an
adept to a counting-post.

Then she found the hatch.

What is under this house is a fiendish hive, and she has decided — without evidence, without a
single spell that would let her do it, and with the total conviction of somebody who needed this
to be true — that the Lord of the Locust Host put it there **for her**. The nest is hers to
command. It is the reason she has stopped counting anything. The ledger's tallies run out three
weeks ago.

**She cannot be bribed with money.** She is paid better than the men and knows what that is
worth. Offer her coin and the hook comes up an inch.

**What she wants is to be believed.** That is the lever, and it is a strange one. She has had no
one to tell for a year. A party that engages with the claim — that asks how she commands them,
how many there are, what the Lord asked of her — will get far more out of her than one that
threatens her, because nobody has taken her seriously since she started saying it.

**She will not turn on Deskari** and the party should not waste a scene trying. She will trade
the Labyrinth without hesitating; that was never hers.

**The muster list in her pocket is the whole story.** Nineteen names, eleven struck through, none
added in a long while, and the last four strikes in a different ink from the rest. She struck
them herself. Every man who argued with her about the hatch, or tried to walk south in the night,
went down it — and the eleventh was three weeks ago, which is also when she stopped counting
patrols. She does not regard this as murder. She regards it as **offering**, and she will say so
levelly, in the same voice she uses for the weather, if anybody asks.

**She is not sure any more.** Play this underneath everything else. Eleven men have gone down
that hatch and not one of them came back up with an answer, and somewhere in her there is a woman
who has begun to wonder whether she has simply been feeding people to animals. She will not admit
it. But a party that gets her talking, and then asks the quiet question — *has it ever once done
what you told it?* — has found the only thing that frightens her.
