## Description

> A lean woman in her thirties, dark hair scraped back hard and pinned, pale eyes that have
> already finished looking at you. Plain studded leather over dark wool, a heavy mace at her belt
> and an iron hook lashed over her left hand with cord worn into the wrist. When she does not
> believe you, that hand comes up an inch without her deciding to.
>
> **On her head is a crown.** It is a band of beaten copper gone green at the edges, with two
> long brass antennae standing up from the front on coiled springs, each ending in a little brass
> ball. When she turns her head they sway. When she nods they bob. She wears it with total
> gravity, and the whole of her face dares you to say something.

## Special abilities

**Adept spells, CL 2nd, concentration +5.** The full set is on the Spells tab and the cast
buttons work; these are the two that change the fight.

- ***Burning hands*** — 1d4 fire in a 15-foot cone, Reflex DC 14 for half. Indoors, under a roof,
  with the party bunched in a doorway, this is the most dangerous thing in the house.
- ***Cure light wounds*** — on herself, at about half, and early rather than heroically late.

**The crown does nothing.** This matters and it is not on the sheet. It is a mummer's prop, the
Locust King's crown from a Sarkorian harvest play, found in a chest in this house and older than
anyone in it. *Detect magic* finds nothing. When Ysolde commands the ants and they obey, it is
because **Nib**, invisible on her left shoulder, repeated the order (see **Voice of the Brood**).

## Tactics

She is a sergeant, not a duellist, and she fights like one.

- **She sets the ground before initiative.** Given warning, she puts the squatters in the doorway
  and stands behind them where she can see the whole room.
- **She opens with the cone** if the party obliges by bunching, and holds it for several rounds
  rather than waste it on one target.
- **She calls the ants.** One hand to the crown, a command spoken aloud (*"Rise. Rise, and
  feed."*), and if Nib is with her, the trapdoor bangs open and soldiers come up out of the
  cellar on the next round: **1d2 soldiers, once**, and none at all if the clutch has been burned.
- **She does not close.** The mace is for when something reaches her.
- **She heals herself, not them.**
- **Losing, she goes for the hatch**, not the open country. She goes down believing she is
  retreating into her own house. Whether the ants agree is up to Nib.

## Roleplaying

### What she is

She serves **Deskari**, Lord of the Locust Host, and she served him quietly inside the
Ivory Labyrinth's counting-post, as its adept, while the arcanist **Ostrander Gell** ran it. The
men wear **Baphomet's** maze. The two lords are allies, and nobody in the Labyrinth thought twice
about attaching a Deskari adept to a post that only had to count.

**Then Gell went down the hatch and did not come back**, and the little voice that had been
whispering to her in the night for a month came out from behind the chimney and told her what
was under the house, and who it was for.

### What she believes

**That she was chosen.** That the Lord of the Locust Host sent a queen to this house for her, and
a herald to tell her so, and a crown to command the brood with. That the ants obey her. That she
is digging Deskari a road to Drezen, under the wall and up into the city, and that when it is
finished she will be remembered for it.

She kept the counting going for most of a year after Gell fell, because the pay came along the
markers only while the tallies went back. **Three weeks ago she stopped.** The tunnel was going
well, and the herald said the Labyrinth did not matter any more. The Labyrinth's men are still
useful for one thing.

**The muster list in her pocket is the whole story.** Nineteen names. Eleven are struck through:
every man who argued with her about the hatch went down it. Two more, Garth Wyle and Tam Ricket,
are crossed out with a different mark and a word beside them, *deserted*, in fresh ink. She does
not regard any of this as murder. She regards it as **feeding**, and she will say so levelly, in
the same voice she uses for the weather, if anybody asks.

### How to play her

**Absolutely serious, about a hat with antennae on it.** That is the whole performance. The
company will laugh, probably out loud, and she will let them, and then she will wait until they
have finished. Nobody has laughed at her in a year and she does not think it is funny. The horror
of her is that she is not ridiculous to herself in any way.

- **She talks to nobody.** In any conversation that lasts more than a minute she will stop and
  listen to something on her left, and answer it aloud: *"Yes. I know. I know they are."*
- **She cannot be bribed with money.** Offer her coin and the hook comes up an inch.
- **What she wants is to be believed.** A party that engages with the claim (how she commands
  them, how far the road has gone, what the Lord asked of her) gets far more out of her than one
  that threatens her. She has a great deal to say about the road, and she is proud of it.
- **She will not turn on Deskari.** She will trade the Labyrinth without hesitating; that was
  never hers.

### The quiet question

**She is not sure any more.** Play this underneath everything. Eleven men went down that hatch
and the brood grew, which is what she was promised. But she has never once been alone with the
ants. The herald is always there.

A party that has worked out what Nib is, and asks her the quiet question (*"Have you ever given
them an order when it wasn't there?"*), has found the only thing that frightens her. She will not
answer. Her hand will go up, very slowly, to her left shoulder.

**If Nib is gone** (killed, driven off, or caught out), Ysolde gives the brood an order, and
nothing happens. She gives it again, louder. Let it be quiet for a long time. Then she takes off
the crown, and looks at it, and the company should not need to be told anything else about her.
