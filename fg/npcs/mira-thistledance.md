## Description

> A half-elf woman with green eyes and brown hair threaded with fine silver chains comes at you
> across the room already talking, a lute swinging on its strap behind her. She has a wax tablet
> in one hand, covered edge to edge in cramped notes and arrows, and she looks at you the way
> somebody looks at a present they have been waiting all year to open. *"There you are. Sit, sit.
> Nobody followed you? Of course nobody followed you. Wine? It's good wine. I have a question for
> all of you and I need you to think about it very seriously."*

## Special abilities

- **Inspire courage +2** (bardic performance, *Lingering Performance*): +2 morale on attack and
  weapon damage rolls and on saves against charm and fear, for every ally who can hear her lute.
  It is the one thing she does in a fight, and she does it very well.
- **Spells** as an 8th-level bard. She has *good hope*, and she casts it the moment anybody she
  likes looks worried.

## Tactics

**She does not fight for the party**, and she will not come with them; she has a network to run,
and in the stories the spymaster never leaves the web. If a fight comes to her, she plays. She
stood at the back of every fight from Drezen to the Sarian Fane with the crusade's mythic four,
and she is braver than she looks and much better with a lute than a rapier.

## Roleplaying

### Who she is now

**Lupenor Celest left Mira Thistledance to keep her trading house and, almost in passing, to
listen.** When the four of them realised how porous their compounds were, they agreed Mira should
hold the thread of what the city was saying — she is in every tavern in Drezen anyway. *Not spies,*
they kept insisting to one another. *Nothing so grand.* Servants and pot-boys and stall-hands who
hear things, a few silver here and there, and someone sensible to write it all down.

Then they rode west, and Mira was left alone with the job, and **a lifetime of ballads and
adventure novels had been waiting for exactly this moment.** She is not keeping notes. She is
running a *network*. She has *assets*. She has a wall with string on it. She is, in her own quiet
and entirely private estimation, **the spymaster of Drezen**, and she is having the time of her life.

**Play her bright, warm, fast and bubbling over.** She is delighted to see the party every single
time. She talks with her hands. She leans in to whisper things that do not need whispering. She
says *operative*, *asset*, *dead drop*, *cover story* and *the network*, all with evident relish and
not always correctly. Her enthusiasm is completely sincere and completely infectious, and it is
the whole point of her: this campaign's employer is not a cold hand pulling strings, she is the
person in the room most excited that the party exists.

### What she is not

**She is not wise, not a mastermind, and not a particularly good strategist.** Play against every
instinct to make her the calm, all-knowing handler. She is a clever woman and a good bard — she
has solved problems in a fight that stumped the mythic four — but she has never run anything like
this, and **she is not sure about the details.**

- **Her information is good about people and bad about everything else.** She knows who is talking
  in which tavern, who is sleeping where, and what the pot-boy at the Brass Lantern heard. She
  does not know how many are in a cell, what is under a floor, or what anything means. When she
  guesses, she guesses from stories, and says so without noticing.
- **Her plans come from ballads.** Asked how the party should approach something, she will suggest
  going in disguised as a troupe of acrobats, or a laundry delivery, or monks. **She drops her
  plan instantly and with open admiration** the moment a player offers a better one — *"Oh, that's
  much better. That's* much *better. See, this is why I need you."*
- **She never solves the problem.** She is a source of money, of warmth, of rumour, and of a job.
  The thinking is the party's, and she is visibly thrilled every time they do it.

### The strike force

**Seeing the four of them in the market convinced her that she has found her elite squad of spies
and saboteurs**, and she has made up her mind to build them into one. She tells them so, early
and often. She believes, because every story she has read says so, that the best operatives
belong to no one — no order, no company, nothing that leads back to her. That is why she wanted
people who were unaffiliated, and it is the genre talking, not calculation. If the party ever
works out that "deniable" also means "nobody would miss you", **she is stricken**: she had not
thought of it that way, and she apologises at length, and then, a little guiltily, asks whether
they think that is the sort of thing a real spymaster *would* think of.

She gives each of them a **codename** at the first meeting, on the spot, and they are not good.
Let the players counter-propose, and let her accept the counter-proposals gratefully. She uses
the codenames in public, loudly, which rather defeats them.

### The name

**She has not yet found the PERFECT name for the strike force, and she is determined to.** Every
time the party meets her, she presents a few new candidates, reads them aloud with feeling, and
then asks what the party thinks and whether they have any ideas of their own. **Write down every
name a player suggests.** At the next meeting she brings back the best of them, improved with at
least one unnecessary word, alongside her own new attempts. She never quite commits — every meeting
ends with *"We'll keep thinking"* — until the party champions one name wholeheartedly, and then she
adopts it with tears in her eyes and never lets it go.

Her candidates, meeting by meeting:

- **The back room, after the market:** *The Quiet Knives. The Market Shadows. Operation
  Nightingale* — *"No, wait, that's for a mission, isn't it. Save it. Save that one."*
- **Payment, after the South Bank:** *The Unaffiliated* (*"Because you're not affiliated.
  Officially."*). *The Cinder Row Irregulars. The Lantern and the Knife.*
- **The briefing for the manor:** *The Nightjars. The Fifth Column* — somebody should tell her
  what a fifth column is; she is appalled, and then decides she likes it anyway. *The Grey
  Lanterns.*
- **Spares, for any later meeting:** *Thistle and Thorn* (her own name, and she goes pink).
  *The Drezen Irregulars. The Whisper Company. Mira's* — she stops, and crosses it out.

### The questions that come up

- **Can she be bribed?** She does not need to be. She will tell the party nearly anything they
  ask, including things she should not, because they are *her* operatives.
- **What does she want?** To be good at this — for the network to matter to the crusade, for
  Lupenor to come home and be proud of what she built. And to find the name.
- **Is she lying to them?** Never on purpose. She is simply wrong about things fairly often, and
  sure of herself when she is.
- **What happens when a job goes badly?** She is upset for them, not with them. She takes it hard
  and briefly, and then she has a new idea, and it is from a ballad.
