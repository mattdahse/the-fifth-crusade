## Description

> A narrow man of about fifty in a chain shirt that fits him the way a borrowed coat fits a boy,
> over a clerk's black sleeves gone grey at the cuffs. Ink on the fingers of both hands. A glaive
> leans on his shoulder like a mop somebody asked him to hold. He is already talking, and what he
> is saying is that you have been written down.

## Special abilities

**Adept spells, CL 3rd, concentration +5.** Three first-level spells, and every one of them is a
way out of a room.

- ***Obscuring mist*** is the only thing in the Low Road that beats darkvision: fog blocks it as
  surely as it blocks a lantern. He casts it and runs, which is the whole plan.
- ***Command*** (Will DC 13): *"Halt."* *"Drop."* *"Kneel."* He uses it the way he uses
  everything, to get one round further from whoever is holding the axe.
- ***Cause fear*** (Will DC 13) on whichever of them looks most likely to catch him.

**The Keeper's glaive** is carried because the rule says the Keeper carries one. He is not
proficient and holds it like a mop. It is on the sheet at -4 because he will try it exactly once.

**The key ring.** Fourteen keys, none labelled. Finding the strongroom key on it takes him **1d4
rounds**, and anybody else who takes the ring takes the same.

## Tactics

- **He does not fight.** He has *Run* and he uses it: five times his speed down a tunnel he
  cannot navigate, which is how he ends up in the wrong room.
- **Opening move:** *obscuring mist* on himself, then away. If there is nowhere to go, *command*
  on the nearest weapon.
- **He never stands beside the Horn.** They are not on speaking terms.
- **Touch him and he surrenders.** He is surrendering while he casts; he is surrendering while he
  runs. The moment somebody has hold of his sleeve, he is offering terms.
- **At dusk he goes for the ladder** with the strongbox and the ledger, and he goes alone. See the
  Keep page for the clock.

## Roleplaying

He counted wagons for a merchant house in Kenabres for twenty years and took the maze because the
Labyrinth paid better for the same work. When Drezen fell they sent him to keep the Way, and the
order has written to him once since: a reminder of the correct hymns for the Feast of the Horned
Month. It is framed.

**He believes he is the Master of the Drezen Way** because he holds the ledger, the keys and the
strongroom, and because the note that came up the ladder three weeks ago was addressed to a title,
and he is the only one down here who knows what a title is. He has written to the order for a
ruling three times. The letters are in his desk, because nobody has gone west to carry them.

**The ledger is false.** The rats have eaten a third of the grain and he has entered it under
*Spoiled* and *Sent West* in equal parts, so that the columns come out. Nobody in the order has
ever read it. He knows that and keeps it straight anyway.

**He is packed.** A bag under the cot in the counting room holds a change of clothes, the ledger's
fair copy and a purse. At dusk he means to take the strongbox down the ladder and leave the Horn
holding the Gate. The bag is the lever: show it to Gerrit Bask and the quarrel ends in the Horn's
favour at once.

- **Can he be bought?** With his life, immediately, and with his ledger, which is worth more to
  him than his life. He will trade the Horn's position, the stores, the route, the dressing
  room's register and the manor ("Ysolde Karn's post, two days west; they stopped writing three
  weeks ago") for a door out and his book.
- **What he wants:** to be told he did the paperwork correctly. Tell him, and he will tell you
  anything.
- **Looking for an excuse to stop?** Always. He was looking for one before the party arrived.
- **Will he turn on the people he works for?** In a sentence, and he will offer to put it in
  writing.

He has already entered the party in the ledger, under *Arrivals, unscheduled: four*, from Chin
Whiskers' description. He would like to know their names, for the column.
