## Description

> A thin young man in a wax-spotted apron is standing on a crate by the pump in the middle of the
> street, shouting. He has a crowbar in one hand, and in the other he holds up something small
> and yellow, the way a priest holds up a relic. His voice keeps cracking. *"He came to Kenabres
> and nobody stopped him! Nobody! You want to see what wins? Look!"* The lid of the crate under
> his feet is already off.

## Special abilities

None. He has **Run** and **Fleet**: speed 35 ft., and 175 ft. in a round flat out, keeping his
Dexterity bonus to AC while he runs. Nobody at the table can match that on foot, and it is the
only thing that makes him hard to catch.

## Tactics

**Surprise round: he kicks the crate over.** That is his entire plan, and it happens before
anybody rolls initiative. Four giant centipedes pour out of it into the crowd.

**Round 1: he preaches.** He stays up on the crate shouting about Deskari and Kenabres, because
he genuinely expected something to *happen* — a sign, a voice, a swarm out of the sky — and
nothing does. A character who closes on him this round can take hold of him before he runs.

**Round 2 onward: he runs.** The moment anybody comes at him with a weapon, or the first
centipede dies and it is plain the Locust Lord is not coming, he bolts for **the alley he carried
the crate up**, on the south side of the street. He ducks under tarps, jumps rugs, and pulls
baskets over behind him. He does **not** fight unless cornered,
and cornered he waves the dagger more than he uses it.

**Catching him is a chase, not a fight.** He outruns anyone on foot. What stops him is a trip, a
grapple, a net, *grease* or *sleep*, a handcart shoved across his path, or somebody who thought
to stand in the alley mouth before he got there. Once he is into the alley he is in the lanes
running down to the South Bank and gone, until Mira's people bring him back that evening. That is the soft failure, and the story
record covers it.

**He surrenders the instant he is held.** A grapple, a successful trip, or any hit that knocks
him down, and he curls up and cries. There is no fight in him.

## Roleplaying

**He is not a cultist. He wants to be one, and nobody will have him.** He was apprenticed to a
chandler in Kenabres and was there in Arodus when the city was broken open. He saw the demon lord
in the sky above it, as everyone who lived did, and he saw the crusade fail to stop it. His
master died in the fire. He came up the road to Drezen on a relief cart with nothing, and
somewhere on that road he decided that if Deskari is what wins, he would rather belong to
Deskari.

No real cult wants a loud, grieving twenty-year-old who tells everyone what he is. **He went
looking for one on Cinder Row**, because every rumour in the South Bank says there is something
under a scrap yard there. He got as far as the shop counter. A big man with a scrap axe told him
that the people he was looking for do not take locusts, laughed at him, and sold him an empty
crate for two coppers to be rid of him. Jory filled it himself over three weeks, with centipedes
out of the South Bank cellars fed on rats, and moulded them a god out of candle wax.

**What he wants now is to have mattered.** Taken alive he talks, all of it, in a flood: Kenabres,
his master, the road, the yard, the man with the axe, the laugh. He is ashamed and defiant in the
same breath and cannot hold either for long.

**He cannot be bribed and does not need to be.** He will tell anyone who listens. What he cannot
tell them is anything past that counter, because he never got further. He confirms the yard is
real and that someone there knew exactly what he meant, and nothing about the house, the numbers,
or what is under the floor.

He is **redeemable**, and worth keeping alive for that as much as for what he knows. What happens
to him next is settled in Mira's back room: she has ideas, most of them from books, and in the end
she does what the party says.
