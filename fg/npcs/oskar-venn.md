## Description

> A narrow, stooped man of about forty-five with flour to both elbows and a clean apron he is
> plainly proud of. His handcart is heaped with round brown loaves under a white cloth, and a
> small girl is sitting on the step of it, swinging her feet. When anyone large comes near, he
> smiles too quickly and says *sorry* before they have said anything at all.

## Tactics

- **He goes for Pip.** On the first round he drops everything and dives for the handcart, and
  gets himself wedged half under it. From then on he is *in* the square beside the cart, prone,
  reaching, and in the way of anyone else trying to get to her.
- **He does not fight.** If a centipede comes for Pip he puts his arm in front of it, and that
  is the whole of his tactics.
- A character who tells him *"I've got her, go"* in a voice he believes gets obeyed at once.

## Roleplaying

**He came up from Nerosyan behind the supply wagons**, two weeks after the city fell, because an
army needs bread and a widower with a child needs work. He rents a cold bread oven in an empty
house two streets over, and he is fiercely, quietly proud that his is the first bread baked in
Drezen for seventy years. Say that to him and he will talk for as long as anyone lets him.

**He is frightened of everything large, and he is trying very hard not to be.** Dorogh makes him
nervous because Dorogh is a half-orc in a helm, not because of anything Dorogh does, and he knows
that is unfair and it makes him more nervous still. If Dorogh is the one who brings Pip out from
under the cart, Oskar will never be nervous of him again, and will say so, badly, with a loaf.

- **What he wants:** a city a child can grow up in.
- **Bribe?** He has nothing worth the trouble. Bread, for ever, to whoever helped: the story
  record already promises that, and he keeps it.
- **What he knows:** he sells to the Preservers' barracks every morning, so he hears which squads
  are out and which are sleeping. He does not think of that as information. Mira would.
