## Description

> A small boy under the table with his hands over his ears, entirely visible from three sides. He
> is not hiding well and he is nine, and those two facts are the same fact.

## Why he has a statblock

**So he is a token in a square.** Three hit points, no attack, Small. He exists on the map so that
an area effect, a charging animal companion or a stray arrow has a visible cost — and so a player
who is about to do something regrettable can see it first.

## Tactics

**He bolts.** That is the entire mechanic and it is worse than standing still.

- **On the first loud thing that happens near him, he runs** — 30 feet, in a direction the GM
  rolls or picks, and *not* sensibly. Through the fight is a legitimate result. Into the shop is
  a legitimate result.
- **After that he keeps moving each round** until somebody catches him, his sister reaches him, or
  he gets out of the building.
- **A character can catch him**: a grapple against CMD 9, or simply a round spent blocking a
  doorway. Nobody has to hurt him to stop him.
- **He is Small**, so he can be picked up and carried by anyone with a free hand.

The design intent is plain: **the party cannot control this room without spending actions on it.**
Spending them is the correct play and costs them the fight tempo. That is the trade.

## Roleplaying

He does not know what is under the house. He thinks the men in the cellar are lodgers, because
that is what he was told, and he will say so to anyone who asks — which is how a party learns that
his sister was told something different.
