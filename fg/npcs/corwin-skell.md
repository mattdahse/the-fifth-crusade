## Description

> A big man gone lean, in a mail shirt that fits him and a coat that does not. He carries a
> longspear like a man who was taught, which he was — the crusade taught him, before he stopped
> being theirs. His head is shaved to the scalp and there are old burn scars up the left side of
> his neck and jaw in a pattern that is not accidental: somebody laid a brand there, more than
> once, and it is the shape of a locust.
>
> He watches the doorway you came in by, and then he watches the doorway on the far side of the
> hall, the one the Labyrinth's men use. He does that a lot.

## Special abilities

None. He is a trained soldier with a spear and reach, and at this level that is quite enough.

## Tactics

He fights like a crusader, because he was one, and it is the most dangerous thing about him.

- **He uses the reach.** Longspear, ten feet, and he keeps a doorway or a table between himself
  and whoever he is fighting. He will give ground to keep the reach rather than to survive.
- **He puts the Locust-Sworn in front.** They are not a screen he regrets spending.
- **He does not commit early.** If the party fights the Labyrinth first, Corwin's people hold
  their rooms and *watch* — which is exactly what they have been waiting a month to do.
- **He will not chase.** Everything he does is about holding the east end of the manor, because
  the east end is where the sealed room is.

## Roleplaying

He served the crusade, took a wound at Kenabres that should have killed him, and came out of it
believing something had spared him for a purpose. **Deskari** obliged with a purpose. He has been
branding himself since.

**He came here for the hive**, and he was here before the Labyrinth's counting-post was. He knows
what is behind the rubble in the north-east room, he believes the Lord of the Locust Host put it
there for him, and he has been quietly trying to reach it for a month — which is difficult with
six of somebody else's people in the building.

**The truce is arithmetic, not friendship.** Three of his against three of theirs, in a manor too
big for either to hold. Neither side can win a fight without losing enough people that the ants
finish the rest. So they split it: the Labyrinth has the kitchen, the store and the hall's west
end; Corwin has the pallets, the table room and the east end. Nobody crosses. Nobody has for
three weeks.

**He is hoping something happens to them**, and this is his whole strategy. Not a plan — a hope.
He wants the Labyrinth's post to be destroyed by somebody who is not him, and a party of armed
strangers walking in through the breach is the most promising thing that has happened to him all
year.

**So he will talk.** This is the important thing about Corwin and the party should get the chance
to find it: he will not attack strangers on sight. He will let them in, tell them where the
Labyrinth's people sleep, offer to stay out of it, and mean every word — right up until the
Labyrinth is dead and the party is between him and the sealed room.

**He cannot be bought**, because what he wants is not for sale and the party has already given it
to him by existing. He can be *used*, easily, by a party that works out he is using them.
