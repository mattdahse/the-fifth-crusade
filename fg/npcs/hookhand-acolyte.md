## Description

> A lean woman in her thirties, dark hair scraped back hard and pinned, pale eyes that have
> already finished looking at you. Plain studded leather over dark wool, no colours, no badge. A
> heavy mace at her belt. An iron hook is lashed over her left hand with cord worn into the
> wrist, and when she does not believe you, that hand comes up an inch without her deciding to.

## Special abilities

**Adept spells, CL 2nd, concentration +5.** The full set is on the Spells tab and the cast
buttons work; these are the two that change the fight.

- ***Burning hands*** — 1d4 fire in a 15-foot cone, Reflex DC 14 for half. Indoors, under a roof,
  with the party bunched in a doorway, this is the most dangerous thing in the house. She opens
  with it if two or more targets line up and she is not already in melee.
- ***Cure light wounds*** — she uses it on herself, at about half, and she uses it early rather
  than heroically late.

## Tactics

She is a sergeant, not a duellist, and she fights like one.

- **She sets the ground before initiative.** Given warning, she puts the squatters in the doorway
  and stands behind them where she can see the whole room.
- **She opens with the cone** if the party obliges by bunching. If they do not, she holds it, and
  she will hold it for several rounds rather than waste it on one target.
- **She does not close.** The mace is for when something reaches her, not for reaching.
- **She heals herself, not them.** The squatters are replaceable and she has a muster list in her
  pocket that says so in her own handwriting.
- **She retreats last and cleanly.** Beaten, she goes for the cellar door or the road, and she
  does not drop the slate.

## Roleplaying

She took the name Hookhand when she was given a warband, which tells you she chose to be a thing
people describe rather than a person they know. The hook is a poor weapon and an excellent
argument, and she has needed to use it as a weapon perhaps twice.

**She cannot be bribed with money.** She is paid better than the men and she knows exactly what
that is worth. Offer her coin and the hook comes up an inch.

**She can be bought with something else.** Nineteen names on the muster list, eleven struck
through, none added in a long while, and the last four strikes in different ink — she is running
out of people and nobody is sending more. What she wants is a way to stop spending them. A party
that works out she is not defending this house so much as failing to leave it has real leverage,
and it is not leverage they can buy.

**She will not turn on the Labyrinth**, and the party should not waste a scene trying. But she
will trade — what is under the house, who is expected on the road, which way the muster went —
for her people walking out. She will keep that bargain, and she will expect it kept, and she is
the sort who comes back later to see whether it was.

**If the squatters break and she does not**, that is the moment worth playing. She has watched
this happen before. She is not surprised, and she is not going anywhere, and that is the most
frightening thing about her.
