The country the manor sits in, for planning an approach. Roughly four hundred yards corner to
corner, with the manor small and right of centre and its interior deliberately not shown — this
is the plate to put in front of the players before they commit to a direction.

## What is on it

- **The manor**, small, and seen as what it actually is from outside: **a slate roof.** A straight
  ridge, two ragged holes where slates have gone through, and **two chimneys at the west end** —
  which is where the two flues actually are on [the battlemap](the-manor.md): the kitchen's brick
  oven in the north-west room, and the great hall's hearth in the west wall. The players cannot
  see inside it, because from four hundred yards away nobody can.
- **The ravine**, west, where the ground simply stops at a pale broken rim and goes dark. Sheer
  enough to matter: nothing approaches from that side.
- **The vanished village** — a dozen low foundations scattered across the ground, knee high and
  half swallowed. They are the only cover on the map, and a party that reads them as cover is
  reading the map correctly.
- **A dry stony watercourse** running down from the east. A wash, not a path: loose rock, no
  water, and low enough to move along unseen if you are willing to be slow.
- **A copse of leafless trees** in the north-east, the only thing on the map tall enough to hide
  a standing man.
- **Clusters of thorn scrub**, a broad drift of pale ash in a hollow, fields of scree and
  boulders, and patches of dead yellow grass. The ground is not uniform, and which way they come
  should be a choice between real options.
- **One dead tree** hard by the manor's east side, and a standing stone to its west.

## The roof, and why the battlemap contradicts it

It does not. The manor **keeps most of its roof** — that is why a fight inside it has no long
bowshots and why the acolyte's *burning hands* is so dangerous under it. This plate shows the
building as anyone outside sees it: a closed grey roof.

[The manor battlemap](the-manor.md) shows the rooms because a battlemap is a **cutaway**. That is
the ordinary convention and no player will trip over it, but it is worth knowing the two plates
are saying different things on purpose rather than disagreeing.

## There is no road

Not on the plate, and not in the world. Whatever was built through this country was erased
generations ago by demons, by Worldwound weather, and by armies going both ways over it. *"The
road"* in this commission is the name of a direction, not a thing anybody can stand on, and the
[quest](../quests/hold-the-halfway-house.md) says so outright.

Which is why the manor matters at all: with nothing to steer by and nowhere else to sleep, every
courier crossing this country ends up passing within sight of it.

## No grid and no occluders

Deliberate on both counts. Four hundred yards has no useful five-foot squares, and line of sight
out here is a conversation rather than a calculation — the questions are *can they see us coming*
and *how close can we get to that wall*, and both are better answered by looking at the plate than
by FG tracing a wall. The tactical layer starts at [the manor](the-manor.md).
