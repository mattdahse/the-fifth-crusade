Where the stores are made up into loads for the west: six porter's loads lashed in a row, three
small casks, a crate or two, and two people doing the lashing.

> A woman in a porter's leather yoke has a sack halfway onto her shoulders and her hands still on
> it. A younger man beside her is holding a coil of cord. They look at you, then at the sack, and
> the woman puts it down very carefully, as though it might be evidence.

**Nell Carrow and her brother Sim**, South Bank, paid a sack of grain a night by the Keeper through
Hesk. They believe he is a grain merchant avoiding the market's fees because it is convenient to
believe it. **They do not fight.** They put things down and sit against the wall.

- **For the same wage**, a sack a night, they will carry the grain *back*, and they will be
  delighted.
- **What they know:** the loads go west through the gate at dusk; the Keeper and the Horn have not
  spoken in three weeks; the Horn is kinder; the rats have eaten more than has gone west, and the
  Keeper writes it down anyway.

**Lamp oil.** The three casks are stencilled with the market's mark too. There is no lamp oil on the
South Bank, a fact the whole quarter knows by heart; there has been lamp oil under it all along.

**Dorogh's birthday present.** In one of the market's crates, wrapped in straw, is a painted music
box: two dancing cranes on the lid, eleven notes of a tune. On the morning of the 26th Dorogh came
into the market to buy a birthday present and had not settled on whose. This one is stolen, from
the market he was shopping in. What he does about that is his to decide.

**The stockings.** A crate with a crusade quartermaster's stamp holds twelve pairs of good wool
stockings, in a city where everyone's clothes are ruined. Somewhere in the citadel there is a column
headed *Shrinkage*, and this is in it.

**Where it goes:** west to the grain store. Nowhere else.
