The passage narrows and comes out in a cave in the cliff face, open to the air. A rope ladder is
spiked to the stone at the lip and heaped on the floor beside it, ready to go over.

> The passage ends in daylight. The cave mouth opens on nothing at all: sixty feet of air, and then
> broken ground at the foot of the cliff, and the cemetery, and the Marchlands going away grey to
> the west. Four iron spikes are driven into the stone at the lip and a rope ladder is made fast to
> them and heaped in a coil, rungs and all. The walls of the cave are covered in names.

**The names.** Hundreds of them, scratched into the rock at the height of a person sitting down to
wait for dark: first names, mostly, some with a village, some with nothing but a mark. **Every name
here chose the Marchlands.** The last swallow in the Low Road is here too, above the lip, in flight.

**The slaves had no ladder.** They had a rope, and one ring that let a body fall slowly. There is a
groove worn in the rock beside the spikes where a cord ran for seventy years, hauling the ring back
up for the next one. **Perception DC 12** to find it; Emmet Rook will explain it.

**The cemetery below** has mourners in it all day, which is why nothing uses the ladder in daylight.
It is the reason for the dusk clock (see the Low Road page).

- **Kicking the ladder over is a move action**; it falls its full length in the same round. Hauling it
  back up is a full round, and can be done from the top with somebody on it.
- **No Climb check to go down it** at half speed, unhurried. **Climb DC 5** in a hurry, in armour, or
  with a hand full; a **60 ft. fall** (6d6) if one fails. **Climb DC 25** on bare rock.
- **Survival DC 15**, or the marked map, to say where the foot of the cliff comes out.

## At dusk

If the Keeper gets this far he is here with the strongbox in both arms and the ledger in his bag, and
a ladder he has to kick over and climb one-handed. He will offer the party the strongbox for the
ladder, and then the ledger for his life, in that order. **Hauling the ladder up with him on it** is
a choice the party can make, and he will say so, very fast.

**Cutting the ladder down is a permanent change to the city.** Four spikes, a knife and about a
minute, or the whole thing carried off at forty pounds. Either way the Ivory Labyrinth's back door
into Drezen stops working, and the people it walked in can no longer walk out. Say that out loud at
the table. It is a bigger result than anyone in the Low Road.
