*The road fight. Dusk on the first day, at the last marker before the valley.*

The markers lead from the foot of the cliff to the manor, one cairn in sight of the next. The
halfway house is a day's walk out. This happens at the **last cairn**, two or three hours short of
it, with the light going. That timing matters. It puts the party in front of the manor at
dawn, tired and possibly hurt, knowing exactly what the deserter told them. That is a much better
planning scene on the approach plate than one they walk into blind.

## Before the fight

Give them the walk. The Marchlands are flat and broken and the colour of a dead fire. Every hour
or so someone sees the next cairn. Twice they pass something that used to be a farm.

Then, mid-afternoon, **Kora or whoever has the best eyes** sees wolves, far off, three of them,
loping along a rise parallel to the markers. They are not coming closer. They are keeping pace.
**Survival DC 12** says they are well fed and in no hurry, and that they keep coming back to
the line of the markers, the way dogs come back to a back door where they have been fed before.

> The last marker stands on a rise above a dry wash, a cairn of grey stones as tall as a man,
> beside the stumps of an old sheepfold whose walls come no higher than your knee. The sun is
> going down red behind it.
>
> There is a man on top of the cairn. He has a rock in his hand.
>
> And in the grass around the foot of the rise, sitting up like dogs waiting to be fed, there are
> three wolves. They have turned their heads to look at you. One of them yawns.

## The ground

- **The cairn**: 10 feet across and 6 feet high. Climbing it is a Climb DC 10 and a move action.
  Standing on top gives the high-ground bonus (+1 on melee attacks against anyone below) and puts
  you next to Garth.
- **The sheepfold**: low stone walls in a broken square, 30 feet on a side, just downhill.
  Partial cover (+2 AC) to anyone crouched behind it. Stepping over a wall costs 5 extra feet of
  movement.
- **The dry wash**: below the rise, stony and loose. Difficult terrain. This is where the wolves
  come from if they flank.
- **The light.** Dusk now, dark in five rounds. The wolves have darkvision. Riven and Kora's
  lights matter, and so do the everburning torches if the party still has them. Something on fire
  makes a fine place to stand with your back to.

**Optional: an ash squall.** If you want it nastier, a grey wind comes up off the Marchlands in
the second round and cuts sight to 30 feet for d4+2 rounds. The wolves do not care. Use it only
if the fight is going too easily.

## The fight

**They let the party close.** The wolves do not run from four armed strangers. They have been
watching a meal since before dawn yesterday and now there are five of them. They get up, unhurried, and move
to put the party between themselves and the cairn.

**Round one.** Two wolves go for whoever is smallest and furthest from Dorogh, usually Diddle,
and try to flank and trip. The third circles wide through the wash.

**Then.** The third comes in on whoever is tripped. A prone character with two wolves on them is
in real trouble, and that is the point: *a yard dog is dangerous.* The wolf that smites saves it
for the first character who visibly prays or glows.

**Garth** throws stones from the top, one a round, at whichever wolf is nearest the cairn. He is
a poor shot. He is very glad to have something to throw at.

**They break** when two are down. The third runs flat out into the dusk, and does not come back
tonight.

### Scaling

- **The party is hurt or a player is missing:** two wolves. CR 3, and still a real fight.
- **The party is cruising after three rounds:** the ash squall, or the third wolf turns out to be
  the bigger one (+4 hp, +1 to hit).
- **Somebody goes down:** the wolves try to drag them. A wolf that drops a character spends its
  next turn pulling them 10 feet toward the wash rather than biting again. This buys the party a
  round, and it is the most frightening thing in the fight.

### XP

1,200 for three wolves, 800 for two. Award it whether the wolves die or run. Garth is not an
enemy and is worth nothing dead.

## After

The sun is down. The man on the cairn sits on the top stone for a while without speaking, and
says something to the empty stones beside him. Then he asks whether anyone has any water.

**He will not come down yet.** First he wants to watch every one of them sweep a hand over both
shoulders and shake out their cloak, slowly, in the last of the light, and Riven's horns make it a
long conversation. His record has the whole
of what the house did to him. Play that before anything else: it is the scene.

**His name is Garth Wyle**, and everything he knows and wants is on his record. The scene is
short and should stay short. The point of it, in order:

1. **He is one of hers.** The maze at his throat. Let the players decide what that means to
   them before he says anything else.
2. **The purse.** If they have the sealed purse marked *Y. K., the halfway house*, he sees it,
   and goes very quiet. It is his pay. He does not ask for it.
3. **The house.** He will draw it in the dirt with the haft of his axe: two holes, no door, the
   hall, the kitchen where the last two men sleep, her room, and the hatch. Then he will tell
   them about the crown, and about the thing on her shoulder, and watch to see whether they
   believe him. He does not know what is under the hatch. *"Whatever's down there, it never sent anyone back
   up."*
4. **Tam.** On the far side of the rise, in the grass the wolves came out of, there is a place
   nobody should look at for long: a coat, what is left of a pack, and a maze-stamped iron disc
   on a broken thong. If anyone brings the disc to Garth, he turns it over in his fingers and
   puts it in his pocket, not round his neck, and tells the empty stone beside him that he has
   it.

**What they do with him is theirs.** Send him south. Send him to Drezen with a note for Mira, the
Gardener's first asset from inside the house, and she will be beside herself. Take his disc and
let him go. Or something worse, which is also a choice people make on the Marchlands. He will
not go back to the house with them under any circumstances.

**They camp at the cairn,** because it is the only high ground for miles, and the third wolf sits
on the next rise all night where they can see it. In the morning it is gone. In the morning,
from the top of the cairn, they can see the valley, and the slate roof with two holes in it.

> Then put **the approach plate** in front of them.
