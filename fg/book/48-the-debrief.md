*Picks up where the October 17 session stopped: Riven has just offered to mind Mira's house while
she fetches the prisoner, and Mira is looking at him.*

The job of this scene is to turn a pile of loot into a direction. Everything the company carried
out of the Labyrinth points west, and by the end of it they should be holding three things: a
reason to care about the halfway house, a commission from **Elara Dawnstrider** to go there, and
a choice about which door they leave Drezen by. Let Mira be delighted for most of it. The one
moment she is not is the moment the table should remember.

## 1. The shed

Mira does not leave Riven alone in her house. She takes Riven *and* Kora to the shed, and on the
way she stops at a bakery shutter, knocks a pattern, and says something to whoever is behind it.

> The shed behind the church is dark and smells of cut stems. Dorogh is sitting on an upturned
> pot with a morningstar across his knees. Diddle is sitting on a sack of bone meal. Anselm is
> sitting where he was put, with his hands folded, the way a clerk sits outside an office he has
> been told to wait for.
>
> Mira looks at him for a long moment, then at the four of you, then at the shed.
>
> *"Same shed,"* she says. *"Same shed. I am going to forgive this, because you brought me a
> bookkeeper, and I have always wanted a bookkeeper."*

She has a cloak for Anselm with the hood already up, and a jar of mustache paste, and she uses
both. **Anselm with a moustache** is the image to hold onto: it goes on crooked and he does not
complain.

They go back to her house by four turnings more than it needs. She calls everybody by codename
the whole way, at a volume that carries: **Nightshade** (Diddle), **Daisy** (Riven), **Lily**
(Dorogh), **Tulip** (Kora).

## 2. The back room

> The back room has changed since you were last here. The wall of red string has spread onto a
> second wall. There is a new card pinned near the middle that says **THE COUNTING HOUSE?** with
> the question mark gone over several times. There is also a new chair, a heavy one, with a
> length of cord already looped round its back.
>
> Anselm looks at the chair. Then at Mira. Then he sits in it without being asked, and holds out
> his wrists.

**She wants everything, in order, twice.** Let the players tell it. She interrupts constantly and
always with the wrong question (*"What colour was the Bishop?"*). When they reach the darkmantles,
she writes *DO NOT GO INTO CAVES* on her tablet and underlines it, as a note for future
operatives.

Then the loot comes out of the packs one piece at a time, and each piece gets its own moment.
Run them in this order. The order is the scene.

### The ledger

The five columns: *Received*, *Sent West*, *Spoiled*, *The Bishop*, and *Shrinkage*.

> Mira turns the pages slowly. Then faster. Then she holds it against her chest with both arms.
>
> *"This is an enemy ledger. An actual enemy ledger. Do you know how many ballads have an enemy
> ledger in them? Seven. I've counted."*

She asks Anselm what *Shrinkage* means. He tells her, with professional precision, that it is
whatever went missing that he could not prove was stolen. She pins a new card on the wall that
says *SHRINKAGE* and draws a string from it to nothing yet.

### The framed letter and the three unsent ones

The hymns for the **Feast of the Horned Month** in **Kuthona**, attendance expected. Mira pins
it up at the top of the wall, highest of anything, because it has a *date* on it, and a date is
a thing you can stop.

The three letters asking the Templars to rule on who is **Master of the Drezen Way**:

> *"The Masters of the Drezen Way,"* Mira says, testing it. Her eyes go somewhere far away.
> *"Oh, that's good. That's very good. Can we have it? Is it theirs? Does it matter that it's
> theirs?"*

She does not decide. It goes on her list (see **The name**, below).

### The register

Save this one. It is the turn.

Fourteen arrivals since Rova. *A cooper; cannot cooper. A laundress; for the market end. A widow,
for the west end; comes and goes by the ladder.* And the eighth and the eleventh, which say only
*For the Preservers*.

> Mira reads the eighth entry, and then the eleventh, and then she reads them again with her
> finger under the words. She sits down. For the first time since you have known her she does
> not say anything at all.
>
> Somewhere outside a cart goes by.
>
> *"I sent for two Preservers,"* she says at last. *"Tonight. On the way to the shed. They're
> coming here."* She looks at the door. *"I don't know which two."*

**This is the scene's one cold moment, and it should land cold.** Iomedae's Preservers are the
newest pillar in Drezen, taking recruits faster than anyone can check them, and two of those
recruits came up a rope ladder. Nobody knows which two. Mira does not either.

If the party found the **Preserver's buckle** in the midden, this is when it goes on the table.
It belonged to a man who came down asking questions in Lamashan. Nobody noticed he was gone.

**What she proposes:** to go on exactly as planned, receive the Preservers, and *watch them very
closely*, because that is what they do in the stories. **What she will take instead:** anything
better. Meeting them at the street door rather than letting them in. Moving Anselm before they
arrive. Sending for **Elara** herself, by name, and nobody else. She drops her plan the instant
she hears one, with open relief: *"Oh, that's much better. See, this is why I need you."*

**How you run it is open.** The two who knock can be honest recruits, and the tension is all in
the room. If you want the thread to run longer, one of them is the eighth entry, and he is very
polite, and he looks at Anselm a beat too long. Anselm notices. He says nothing in front of him
and asks, afterwards, very quietly, whether he could stay with the company instead.

### The back door

Mira asks them to draw it. Hand Riven or Dorogh a stick of charcoal and let them. The cliff, the
ladder, the lip of rock that hides it from the watchtowers.

> *"So that's how they come in."* She traces the line of the cliff with one finger. *"You don't
> close a door like that, you know. Not in the stories. You watch it, and you count who uses it,
> and one day the right person comes up the ladder."*
>
> She stops. *"That's what they were doing. Isn't it. Counting."*

### The tag

Diddle's potion: ***lesser restoration***, tag *H.E. 212* in his own hand, out of the storeroom
behind his master's shop. And the ledger line: *Helmsmith's, potions, four, bought, two hundred
gold.*

Mira is gentle with this, and it is the only time she is careful. She does not accuse
**Helmsmith**. She asks Diddle what he wants to do, and she means it: ask his master himself, let
her people look into it quietly, or leave it alone for now. **Whatever he picks, she does it and
does not argue.** This thread is Diddle's and should stay his until he pulls it.

### The purse and the map

Last. The heavy purse sewn shut and sealed with the maze, marked **Y. K.** and *the halfway
house*. The map on oiled linen, north and west of Drezen, with a line of marks that are not
places.

Mira does not know what the halfway house is. **Anselm does**, and this is where he earns his
chair. Ask him and he answers like a man reading from his own books:

- The halfway house is a **counting-post** on the western road. Nineteen men went out to it the
  summer before last under an arcanist, **Ostrander Gell**. Gell died a year ago, of something the
  reports never named, and since then the pay has gone to the post's adept, **Y. K.** He never met
  her. He paid her.
- He sends their pay out by the ladder every month, sealed, and somebody comes in along the
  markers and collects it.
- **Nobody has collected it for three weeks.** This one has been sitting in his strongroom since
  before the Feast was announced. *"It is,"* he says, *"highly irregular."*

> Mira looks at the map, and at the marks, and at the purse. Then she goes to the wall and
> follows a string right off the edge of it with her finger, to a card nobody has looked at in a
> month. It says **ELARA: RUIN, W. ROAD, HALFWAY. EMPTY??**
>
> *"Oh,"* she says. *"Oh, she's going to hate this."*

## 3. Elara

**Elara Dawnstrider** arrives herself, late, in a plain cloak, alone. She has come because Mira
sent for her by name, or because the Preservers who were sent were turned away at the door, or
simply because Mira's message said *urgent* three times. She is tired, and she has the
look of somebody who has been told today that her order is not what she thought it was.

She reads the eighth and eleventh entries without sitting down. She asks one question (*"Who else
knows about this?"*) and accepts the answer. Then she looks at the oiled-linen map and goes
still.

> *"I have a ruin on that road,"* she says. *"Halfway to the Fane. A company sheltered in it in
> Lamashan and said it was three walls and nothing. I have been trying to find someone to go and
> make it a waystation for a month."* She puts her finger on the last mark. *"That isn't three
> walls."*

Then she makes the offer as it stands in **Hold the Halfway House**: forty gold and the
Preservers' writ, anything found inside is theirs, and a written report she will actually read.
Give her the quest's own line. It is better than anything new:

> *"I can give you forty gold and a writ that says you're mine. I can't give you anyone to come
> with you. If that's not enough, say so now and I'll think better of you than if you say it out
> there."*

What she does **not** say, and does not know: what is actually in the manor. She believes it is a
ruin with somebody's counting-post in it. Leave her believing it.

**Mira's private commission**, after Elara has gone: *"If they're counting, they're writing it
down. Bring me whatever they wrote. Anything with writing on it."* She pays nothing for it, and
says so cheerfully. This is the setup for **The ledger delivered**.

## 4. Pay, and the name

**Pay for this commission is your call.** The terms Mira set at the Ahari bridge were to map,
count heads, find out what is being plotted, and bring back whatever could be carried. They
brought back all of that and a prisoner. Suggested: she pays the second instalment on her word
from the first meeting (*"more for better intelligence"*), and the loot is theirs.

Then, with the purse on the table, **she pitches the name.** Her candidates for this meeting:

- **The Nightjars.** *"They're birds that hunt at night. Nobody's ever seen one. Including me."*
- **The Fifth Column.** Somebody should tell her what a fifth column is. She is appalled, and
  then decides she likes it anyway.
- **The Grey Lanterns.**
- **The Lupenor's Marketplace Irregulars**: the company's own *Marketplace Irregulars* from the
  first meeting, brought back improved with at least one unnecessary word.
- **The Masters of the Drezen Way**, which she found tonight and is not sure she is allowed.

She asks what they think, writes down every name a player suggests, and ends where she always
ends: *"We'll keep thinking."*

**And one more thing.** She has been saving a name since the first morning, for a mission worth
the having. She takes it out now, a little shyly:

> She taps the map. *"This one is Operation Nightingale."*

## 5. Before they go

A few loose ends, any of which can be played or summarised:

- **The safe house.** Mira took the idea and ran with it. She has rented a room above the
  candle-maker with the walrus moustache, on the grounds that nobody would look for spies above a
  man who looks like that. Anselm sleeps there tonight, under guard if the party wants one.
- **The grain.** Enough under the South Bank to feed a good part of the city, and the rats are at
  it. Mira wants it brought up. It is not this job, and it will be less grain when they get back.
- **The glaive** and the chain shirts are still down there. So is the bucket of offal.
- **Level 2.** This is the downtime. Let them shop: rope, first of all, since the company's only
  rope went through a tarp with Lutz.

## 6. Which door

The last beat is a choice, and it is a real one. Put it to them plainly.

**The gate.** They go out the west gate in daylight with Elara's writ. The watch officer writes
their names in a book. Anyone who reads that book, and the Labyrinth has shown it can get people
into the Preservers, knows four people walked out on the western road the morning after the
Labyrinth's keeper vanished.

**The back door.** They go down through the tannery and the tunnels, past whatever the rats have
done with the grain, across the cavern under the everburning torches, through a wall that is not
there, and down sixty feet of rope ladder onto the Marchlands, where nobody writes anything down.
It is how the Labyrinth's own couriers went. The first marker should be in sight from the foot of
the cliff.

Whichever they pick, close on the road:

> The Marchlands open in front of you, flat and grey and broken, under a sky that has not made up
> its mind. There is no road. "The western road" is the name for a direction, and every courier
> who has walked it has walked the line that looked least bad on the day.
>
> Then somebody sees it: a cairn of three stones on a rise, a quarter of a mile off, that is not
> quite the kind of thing that happens by itself. You walk to it. You stand on it. And from
> there, small and patient on the next rise, you can see the next one.
>
> Somebody built this road for counting. You are walking it the other way.
