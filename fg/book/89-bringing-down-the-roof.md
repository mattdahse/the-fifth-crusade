# G6. Bringing Down the Roof

<!-- chapter: The Sarkorian Manor -->
<!-- section: The Warren -->
<!-- order: 6 -->

**The natural ending.** Dorogh reads the roof, Diddle makes the charges, the company sets them on
the two props, lights them, and runs, and the queen's chamber comes down on the queen, the clutch,
and a year of Nib's plans. It should feel like the plan of two specific people, because it is:
Dorogh has Knowledge (engineering), Diddle has Craft (alchemy) and five bombs a day, and somewhere
in the company's packs is the Keeper's copy of ***On Mines and Countermines***, with a moth pressed
at the chapter on shoring.

Run it as a sequence under pressure, in initiative once the queen has seen them.

## 1. Read the roof (Dorogh)

**Knowledge (engineering)**, **+2** with *On Mines and Countermines* to hand. A full-round action
looking up from inside the chamber, or a minute from the mouth before anybody goes in.

- **DC 10:** the roof is loose fill, not rock. This chamber is under the watercourse, and it is
  being *held* up.
- **DC 15:** the two timber props carry it. The earth column is the ants' and carries nothing.
  Charge both props and the roof comes down.
- **DC 20:** set the charges at the **head** of each prop, where the crossbeam meets the roof, and
  it comes down all at once. Getting there is a **Climb DC 10** up the post.
- **Failure:** Dorogh is sure it is the column. Let them charge it. It does nothing but bring down
  dust and make the queen very angry.

**Other ways in:** Knowledge (dungeoneering) DC 18 gives the DC 15 answer. The two Labyrinth men,
if they were talked down, know they set the props in Pharast because the roof was falling in on
the eggs, and will say so.

## 2. Make the charges (Diddle)

**Craft (alchemy) DC 15.** A charge is one of Diddle's daily bombs' worth of reagents and a flask of
oil (or alchemist's fire), packed into a clay jar with a slow-match fuse.

- **Made in advance:** a minute each, and Diddle can take 10. Make them upstairs, in the cellar,
  anywhere quiet. This is the planning beat and it should be rewarded.
- **Made under pressure:** a full-round action and a real roll. **Fail by 5 or more** and the charge
  is a dud. Roll it secretly; nobody finds out until it does not go off.
- **The fuse** is chosen when the charge is made: **short** (it goes off at the end of the lighter's
  next turn) or **long** (at the end of the third round after lighting). Long is safer for the
  person lighting it and gives everything else in the warren three rounds to interfere.
- Diddle needs **two charges**, three if the company wants a spare. A thrown bomb lights a fuse as
  well as a torch does, and Diddle will think of that.

## 3. Set them, wedge them, light them

- **Set:** a standard action in a square next to a prop (or at its head, after the climb, for the
  DC 20 version).
- **Wedge:** a move action, or a **DC 12 Strength check** to do it as part of setting. **A wedged
  charge cannot be carried off** by the workers. An unwedged one can.
- **Light:** a move action with a torch or flint and steel, or a bomb thrown into its square.

## 4. What tries to stop them

- **The workers.** They are tidy. At the end of each round, any **unwedged** charge with a worker
  next to it has a **50% chance** of being picked up and carried 20 feet toward the larder, lit or
  not. This is funny exactly once.
- **The soldiers.** The queen calls the brood when she first sees intruders, and they come in
  through the chamber's mouth. Somebody has to hold that gap while the others work.
- **Nib**, if she is still with the hive. This is her whole future and she knows it. Invisible,
  she flies to a lit fuse and **pinches it out** (a standard action; one fuse a round). A
  character next to the charge gets a **Perception DC 20** to notice the fuse sputter and something
  small and pleased with itself beside it. A **wedged charge can still be snuffed**, so somebody
  guarding a charge is not wasting their turn.
- **The queen** does not leave the mound. Unless a character comes within her reach, she does not
  come to them.

## 5. The roof comes down

**Both props blown in the same round, or the second within a round of the first:** the roof comes
down. Use the Core Rulebook's cave-in rules.

- **The bury zone is the queen's chamber**, everything west of its mouth: **8d6 bludgeoning,
  Reflex DC 15 for half**, and buried. Anyone still in there when it goes is in very serious
  trouble, and that should be clear to the table from the moment the fuses are lit.
- **The slide zone is the first 20 feet of gallery outside the mouth**: **3d6, Reflex DC 15 to
  avoid entirely**, pinned on a failure.
- **The queen and her clutch are buried under tons of gravel and stone.** Do not roll for her. She
  is dead, and so is every egg on the mound, and every soldier and worker in the chamber.

**One prop only:** half the roof comes down on that side. The bury zone is that half of the chamber.
The queen is **pinned at the edge of the fall**: she cannot move, takes a -4 penalty to AC, and is
still very dangerous within her reach. Half the eggs are gone. The fight can be finished, or the
other prop knocked out with a second charge, or with a **DC 22 Strength check** and an iron bar
while she screams.

**The column, or nothing:** dust, a grinding noise, and a queen who now knows exactly who to call
the brood for.

## Afterwards

> The noise goes on for a long time after the dust stops: settling, stone on stone, somewhere
> overhead. Then the rustling in the galleries changes. It does not stop. It slows.

**Above ground**, the dry watercourse sinks into a long, raw hollow that anyone at the manor can see
in the morning, and the house's north-east corner drops a foot with a bang that shakes slates off
the roof. The sealed room's floor goes down the bore-hole.

**Below**, the workers go on working, slower every day. Without a queen the warren dies in a season.
Any soldiers that survived wander the galleries leaderless. **The road east stops where it is.**

**Nib's dream is under the gravel**, and she knows it. If she got away, she is somewhere on the
Marchlands with nothing to bring to Deskari and a very good memory for the faces of the people who
did this to her.

**Award:** the queen's **1,200 XP** however she died, **The Roof Brought Down** (500), and **The
Clutch Burned** (500) if the nursery went too, or was burned on the way.
